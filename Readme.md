# Geopolitical Risk and the US Stock Market: Revisiting the War Puzzle with Recent Conflicts

## Overview

This project studies how US stocks respond to geopolitical risk and the outbreak of armed conflict. It re-tests the so-called **"war puzzle"**: stock prices tend to fall while the risk of war builds, but often *rise* once the war actually starts. It uses recent conflicts from 2022 onward and asks whether the explanation lies in how expected the conflict was. A second part uses the daily Geopolitical Risk (GPR) index to identify which US sectors gain or lose when geopolitical risk rises.

## Motivation

Wars and geopolitical shocks are among the largest sources of uncertainty for financial markets. Earlier research found a consistent but counter-intuitive pattern:

- **Brune, Hens, Rieger & Wang (2015)** document the war puzzle for conflicts since WWII. A rising likelihood of war lowers stock prices, while the outbreak of war raises them. An *unexpected* outbreak, however, lowers prices. Standard explanations do not account for the pattern. These include risk aversion, ambiguity aversion, and expectations of a short war. ([PDF](https://doc.rero.ch/record/332519/files/12232_2014_Article_215.pdf))
- **Rigobon & Sack (2003)** and **Leigh, Wolfers & Zitzewitz (2003)** show that the 2003 Iraq war risk lowered US equity prices. They also show that the effect differed across sectors: energy and gold gained, while airlines, consumer discretionary and IT were hurt. ([Rigobon & Sack](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/the-effects-of-war-risk-on-us-financial-markets.htm) · [Leigh et al.](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w9587/w9587.pdf))
- **Caldara & Iacoviello (2022)** built a news-based Geopolitical Risk index. It allows geopolitical risk to be measured daily and split into *threats* and *acts*. ([AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20191823) · [data](https://matteoiacoviello.com/gpr.htm))

Recent conflicts give a fresh test of these findings. They include Russia's invasion of Ukraine (2022), the Hamas attack on Israel (2023), and the Iran–Israel exchanges (2024–2025). The test is useful because these events differ in how much warning markets had. _[Add 1–2 sentences on why this matters to you / the gap you want to fill.]_

**Contribution:** Rather than simply repeating the war puzzle, this project tests its proposed *mechanism*, which is that the market's reaction depends on whether the outbreak was expected or a surprise.

## Research Questions

1. Does the war puzzle still hold for recent conflicts?
2. Does the stock market react differently to *expected* and *surprise* conflict outbreaks?
3. Which US sectors gain and which lose when geopolitical risk rises? Do geopolitical *threats* and actual *acts* have different effects?

## Hypotheses

- **H1 (risk build-up):** The S&P 500 falls as the risk of conflict builds.
- **H2 (outbreak: expected vs surprise):** When an *expected* conflict breaks out, the S&P 500 rises, as the war puzzle predicts. When the conflict is a *surprise*, the S&P 500 falls.
- **H3 (sector effects):** Defence and energy stocks rise with geopolitical risk. Airlines, consumer discretionary and technology stocks fall.
